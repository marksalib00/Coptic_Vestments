Vestments of the
Deacon

These are the vestments worn by a Coptic Orthodox deacon during the Divine Liturgy. The deacon's role is distinct from the priest's — and his vestments reflect that difference in a very deliberate way.
The Role of the Deacon

The deacon does not stand at the altar as the priest does. He moves — between the altar and the congregation. He leads the people in their responses, directs them when to kneel and when to rise, and serves as the bridge between the clergy and the faithful throughout the liturgy. This role of servant and intermediary goes back to the very beginnings of the church, and his vestments have expressed it for nearly fifteen hundred years.

The Vestments
01
The Tonia طونية
Greek: Sticharion (στιχάριον)
History Meaning

The Tonia is the long white robe worn as the base layer — and it is the one vestment shared by every rank of the clergy. Deacon, priest, and bishop all wear it. It is the oldest vestment in the tradition, appearing in the earliest records of Coptic liturgical practice.

White was required for all clergy from the 4th and 5th centuries onward, connected in scripture to God's own garment in Daniel, to Christ at the Transfiguration, to the angels at the Resurrection, and to the saints in Revelation. It is also the color of the baptismal garment — a reminder that before any rank or role, the one wearing it has been clothed in Christ.

02
The Badrashil بدرشيل
Greek: Orarion (ὠράριον)
History Meaning

The Badrashil is the stole worn on the deacon's left shoulder. It is one of the oldest vestments in the entire Coptic tradition. A 5th century letter by the Egyptian bishop Isidore of Pelusium already describes it — comparing it to the towel Christ wrapped around himself before washing the feet of his disciples at the Last Supper, pointing directly to the deacon's identity as a servant of the liturgy.

The earliest ordination manuscripts consistently place it on the left shoulder of the deacon — and that placement is not incidental. It is the vestment of someone who moves and serves, not of someone who stands still at the altar.

Priest vs. Deacon — The Same Garment, Two Roles
The Priest's Sadra

Worn around the neck, hanging straight down the front. He stands at the altar and offers the liturgy.

The Deacon's Badrashil

Worn on the left shoulder diagonally. He moves between the altar and the people, always serving.

Historically, these two stoles began as the same garment — the difference was only in how each rank wore it. Over time they became distinct, but their shared origin reflects that both priest and deacon serve the same God, just in different ways.

The deacon's vestments are simple by design. Two pieces — the white robe and the stole — carried forward from the earliest centuries of the church. What has never changed is what they communicate: purity before God, and readiness to serve His people.

"The congregation is to see less of the clergy and more of Christ — the true High Priest, present at every liturgy."
— Kyrillos Tadros, Vestments of Glory and Honor (2019)
